Project Overview

Epidural anaesthesia requires a needle to cross the posterior spinal ligaments and stop in a fat-filled space only a few millimetres wide, just before the dura. Clinicians do this almost entirely by feel: bone is recognised as a hard stop, and the epidural space as a sudden loss of resistance (LOR) on a syringe plunger.

This project investigates whether a general-purpose robot arm can make the same two judgements without vision — identifying bone contact, and identifying when the needle tip reaches the epidural space.

The work combined a Python control stack for the open-source 7-DoF OpenArm, two rounds of sensing hardware for each objective, and a focused review of 20 full-text papers and eight supplementary studies.

Sensing in the Load Path

Where the sensor sits decides whether the signal is usable. The arm's joint torques were too far from the needle; load cells placed directly in the needle and plunger load paths separated every trial.

Results

Each objective took two sensing iterations to reach full separation.

Objective 1 · Bone Detection

Joint torques — the arm's own joint torques could not distinguish the printed vertebra from silicone. After contact, joint 2 varied only between −1.15 and −0.99 N·m, and the silicone run produced larger torque swings than the hard run on five of seven joints.

1 kg bar load cell — a redesigned syringe holder put the load cell in the needle load path and separated the printed vertebra from silicone in all 26 insertions (peaks of 2.11 M vs 3.38 M counts; Welch p = 1.7 × 10⁻⁹).

Objective 2 · Epidural Space Detection

Force-sensitive resistor — an FSR on the plunger was not sensitive enough at low force.

100 g plunger load cell — separated a tip occluded by silicone from a tip in the epidural space tube in all 25 trials. The median plunger force over the second half of the stroke separated the classes by 4.68 M counts, with 100% cross-validated accuracy.

Conclusion & Next Steps

Both judgements can be made from force alone — when the sensor sits directly in the relevant load path.

Bone contact and the epidural space were both identified from force alone, with load cells placed directly in the needle and plunger load paths.

Relying on the robot's own motor torques is not sufficient.

Caveat — values are uncalibrated raw counts from a lumbar phantom.

Next — calibration, closed-loop testing on the arm, and layered anatomical phantoms.
